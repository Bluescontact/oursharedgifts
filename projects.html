Projects

Working systems, not case studies.

Each project below is a real, running build. Where a project has public code, documentation, or a live instance, it's linked. Where it's early-stage, that's named too.

Personal System

NESI

NESI takes the raw material of an ordinary day and turns it into something that can be recognized, kept, and eventually shared. Kevin decides what crosses; nothing else does. It runs on a single governing rule: a recognition mechanic never does the recognizing. No worth-score, value-rating, or skill-rank is ever emitted by the system — its only job is to surface and hold tension. That rule is enforced structurally, not just stated: it's cited live inside the system's own code and named as its single operative law.

What it's for

A working practice for turning ordinary days into something that holds — built first for Kevin's own use, and useful anywhere a number would distort the thing it's measuring (education, care work, community platforms).

What's real about it today

A running 2D build is its current interface, with its own instrument suite — conservation checks, threshold sweeps, cold-walk verification by someone who didn't build it. Twelve automated instruments plus stranger-tested sessions, not a design doc.

Applied Writing

Architecture of Coherence & the load-bearing patterns

A working manuscript — Architecture of Coherence — and the pattern library behind it, reduced from 176 archival design patterns down to the subset with confirmed live use: patterns cited by name inside running code, a governing document, or an active build, not just an old log entry.

What it's for

A citable, load-bearing account of the design principles underneath the rest of this work — useful to anyone building systems where a stated principle needs to actually hold under running code, not just read well.

What's real about it today

A full manuscript and a 23-pattern cross-section, each entry sourced to the exact file and line that cites it live — not a claim, a citation.

This is a working paper, not a finished doctrine — if you want to talk through where it applies to your own system, book a call.

Gift & Civic Tools

Field Kits for civic & consumer disputes

A Field Kit takes an unresolved structural mess — a housing dispute, a surveillance proposal, a datacenter siting fight, a defective-product matter, a school-closure decision — and produces a crystallized apparatus set a practitioner-ally can use immediately: a structural audit, a one-pager, a recognition diagram, sample letters and templates, bundled into a single distributable document.

Who it's for

Tenant unions, opposition groups, oversight bodies, and consumer-side authors who need apparatus, not just analysis.

What it produces

A structural audit, a distributable one-pager, a recognition diagram, and ready-to-send templates — built to be handed to someone the same day.

How it's released

Nothing is distributed without a deliberate go-ahead. See Library for what's currently available to read or request.

Gift Economy

OurSharedGifts — a gift-circle ledger

A separate, ongoing project exploring what a public gift economy looks like when it's run as a structural ledger instead of a marketing page — nodes naming real capacity and real need, with debt left structurally unrepresentable. It's a different register from this site on purpose: this site is the funder-facing front door; OurSharedGifts is the gift circle itself, running.
