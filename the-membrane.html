The Membrane

What the circuit declines.

A membrane is selective, not closed. It passes what shares its form. It stops what doesn't — not as a wall but as a structural incompatibility. These are the forms this circuit cannot run.

The Principle

The circuit closes on giving. That isn't preference — it's the mechanism the whole thing runs on. Certain forms of exchange are structurally incompatible with that mechanism. They would change what the circuit is in order to pass through it. The membrane names those forms directly so that arriving here doesn't produce confusion about what's possible.

None of what follows is a judgment on the person or the need. It's a structural description of what this form can and cannot hold.

Forms the Circuit Declines

Open accounting
Any exchange where the tab stays open after it ends. Not because obligation is bad — because this circuit closes on giving. The gift completes when given. An open tab means it hasn't completed. The circuit can give into a closed loop or an open field, but it cannot give into a running ledger.
Kevin as headwaters
Arrangements where Kevin is the origin someone else requires to function. The work can be a node — something that changes how you read your own territory. It cannot be the source someone depends on. A circuit that becomes another person's headwaters stops being a circuit. The dependency has to flow the other direction: the work depends on the person staying capable of doing it.
The pattern as product
"I want to implement this system for my organization." What moves is the pattern — already free, already in the field. What doesn't move is the instance: the specific calibration zero, the lived ground, the body that holds the gate. You can use the patterns. You can build on them. You cannot purchase the instance, because the instance is not separate from the person running it.
Someone else's timeline as the engine
The machine runs on its own cadence. The Governor brakes for cost; the Dispatcher routes by readiness. Urgency injected from outside overrides both. Another person's deadline cannot become the pace-setter for a system that exists to protect the person running it. If your timeline requires that, this isn't the right circuit.
The receiving end that can't close
If the person arriving cannot receive without it becoming a debt — cannot hold a gift without obligation forming — the exchange cannot complete. The circuit is designed to give without creating obligation. If something in the receiver's structure converts the gift into a tab, the circuit and the receiver are incompatible. This isn't a failure of either. It's a mismatch of form.
Transmission before recovery
Deep transmission — giving something that changes the receiver's frame at a structural level — costs the transmitter metabolically. The body needs to metabolize that cost before it can transmit again at that depth. Requests that arrive before recovery is complete are not declined because of unwillingness. They are declined because the instrument isn't available. The circuit can transmit at depth when it's ready; it cannot transmit on demand.

What Passes

Anything that shares the circuit's form. Zero-or-gain. Complete on giving. No tab opened. No dependency created. The field uses what it can use and returns what's useful to the commons — that's the form the circuit recognizes.

If you're not sure whether your engagement fits: The Door has the three-part ask form. The structure of the ask tells you — and Kevin — whether the exchange makes sense before anyone invests more than a sentence in it.
