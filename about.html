About

A builder who publishes the working notes, not just the finished thing.

I work at the intersection of systems design, off-grid living, and gift-economy practice — building small, real, load-bearing tools rather than pitching large speculative ones.

Background

I live full-time in a 1995 school bus I call Nesi — home, workshop, and mobile shop in one. Most days, in whatever time is left, I build systems.

Four of them, mainly:

Personal System

NESI

A personal system that takes the raw material of an ordinary day and turns it into something that can be recognized, kept, and eventually shared. I decide what crosses. Nothing else does.

Framework

The Architecture of Coherence

A framework for reading why some structures hold together and others quietly fail, built around four questions: what's being differentiated, what's connecting, what's bounded, what's architected.

How this works

Everything here runs on one rule: a gift completes when nothing remains outstanding. What I make, I hand over finished. Nothing is tracked. Nothing is owed back.

This page follows the same rule. Read what's useful. Take what applies. You don't owe me a reply, a subscription, or a read of anything else here. If you want to go further, it's at the links above. If not, this already closes clean — nothing's outstanding.

A steward is measured by what remains after they leave.

Where the work is based

Nesi runs on 15kWh of off-grid capacity, paired with an electric motorcycle for daily driving — enough to live, work, and travel without depending on shore power or a fuel fill-up.
