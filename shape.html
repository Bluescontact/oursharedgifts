What makes it rigid

The vertices are capacities, the edges are incapacities. A container is rigid because of what it has made impossible, not what it offers.

Each corner is something a group can do. Each line between two corners is something the group has made impossible. A group holds its shape because of what it has ruled out ahead of time, more than because of what it offers.

Three states

A container doesn't sit still. It is in one of three states.

At rest

Nothing is pressing. There is the most room inside, and many ways to move.

In motion

Load comes on. The shape folds and turns, and the room inside it shrinks.

Under load

It comes down to the four corners and shows what it is. This is where you can see which of the four is there, and which is missing.

So the moment a group is tested is also the moment it can be read. Under load, you can see which of the four it has.
